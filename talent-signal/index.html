The premise

Everyone interviews well. Almost nobody works the way they interview.

Two candidates with the same credentials fail in completely different ways — one because the role needed patience they don’t have, the other because it needed a fight they wouldn’t pick.

Signal measures the behaviour underneath the credential: how someone decides, how fast they move, how they handle a client who is angry, and what happens the week after a deal falls through. It reports what the evidence supports and flags what it doesn’t — including when a candidate has answered the way they think you want.

“The best hires are rarely the best interviewers. They’re the ones whose way of working matches the job you actually have.”
— the case for behavioural screening

Five instruments, one profile

Each framework answers a question the others can’t

Signal is a single assessment scored five ways. Every item feeds more than one lens, which is why twenty minutes produces what would otherwise take four separate tests.

01 / OCEAN

Big Five

The research backbone. Five broad traits with the deepest published evidence base for predicting job performance.

ContributesOpenness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Emotional Stability — each built from three or four workplace facets.

02 / DISC

DISC Style

How the person shows up in a room. Scored from forced-choice blocks, so agreeable answering can’t inflate it.

ContributesDominance, Influence, Steadiness, Conscientiousness, plus a named pattern such as Trailblazer or Anchor.

03 / HPI

Hogan-style scales

The bright-side view: how the person is seen at their best, on the day-to-day, by the people around them.

ContributesAdjustment, Ambition, Sociability, Interpersonal Sensitivity, Prudence, Inquisitive, Learning Approach.

04 / PI

Behavioural drives

Four drives that predict how to manage the person — and one nearest reference profile out of eighteen.

ContributesDominance, Extraversion, Patience, Formality, plus a reference profile such as Captain or Guardian.

05 / Caliper

Job competencies

The performance layer, including a short abstract-reasoning section — the part most personality tests leave out.

ContributesPersuasiveness, Urgency & Drive, Resilience, Service Orientation, Accountability, Abstract Reasoning.

How it works

Twenty minutes in. A decision-grade profile out.

Fifty-four statements about how you work, ten forced-choice word blocks, and six reasoning problems. Progress saves as you go, so a closed tab costs nothing.

Scored five ways at once

Read it honestly

A profile that tells you when not to trust it

Most assessments hand back five confident numbers regardless of how the questions were answered. Signal checks the response set first and puts the verdict at the top of the report.

Candour catches a candidate who denies ordinary human failings. Consistency compares answers that measure the same trait from different angles. Engagement catches straight-lining and a pace too fast to be considered.

Where it belongs in a hiring process

  • Do — use it after a first conversation, to shape the interview and the reference calls.
  • Do — weigh it alongside work history and demonstrated results, never instead of them.
  • Do — share the profile with the candidate. It reads as development feedback, not a verdict.
  • Don’t — screen anyone out on a score alone, or use it as a pass/fail gate.
  • Don’t — treat it as a clinical, medical or diagnostic instrument. It is none of those.

Questions

Straight answers

Is this the actual DISC, Hogan, PI or Caliper test?
No, and it doesn’t claim to be. Those are proprietary, separately normed instruments owned by their publishers. Signal is an independent assessment built on the same public constructs — the five-factor model, the DISC quadrants, Hogan’s bright-side scale structure, PI’s four-drive framework and Caliper’s competency-plus-reasoning approach. Where you need a certified, legally defensible instrument for a specific hire, buy the real one.
How long does it take?
About twenty minutes for most people. There’s no timer. Answers save to your browser as you go, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off.
Where does my data go?
Nowhere. Everything is scored in your browser — there is no server, no account and no database. Your answers live in your own browser storage until you clear them, and the only way a profile leaves your machine is if you export the JSON or copy the share link yourself.
Can candidates game it?
Partly, and that’s expected. The forced-choice section makes candidates commit to a preference rather than agreeing with everything, the candour scale catches the too-good-to-be-true response set, and a consistency check compares items that measure the same trait from opposite directions. When those checks trip, the report says the trait scores should be treated as a ceiling and probed in interview.
Are the norms real?
The norm table is illustrative — a documented set of means and standard deviations that spread scores realistically, not a validated sample. It lives in one file and is designed to be replaced: once you have 150+ respondents in a given role, swap in your own means and the percentile bands become genuinely meaningful for your firm.
Is it legal to use this in hiring?
Personality assessment in hiring is regulated, and the rules vary by jurisdiction. Signal deliberately contains no items about health, disability, religion, age, family status or any other protected characteristic — every item is about workplace behaviour. That said, it has not been validated for adverse impact. Use it to structure interviews and to inform judgement, not as a pass/fail screen, and take local employment-law advice before making it a formal part of a hiring process.
