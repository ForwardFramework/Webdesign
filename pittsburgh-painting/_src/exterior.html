The part nobody else will show you

Our written prep spec

Almost every exterior paint failure is a prep failure, not a paint failure. This spec goes on your quote in writing, so you can hold us to it — and so you can ask the other two bidders which of these steps they’re actually doing.

  • Pressure wash the entire envelope

    Dirt, chalking, mildew and pollen come off first. Paint does not stick to any of them. Then the house dries fully — we don’t coat damp substrate.

  • Scrape and sand every failing edge

    All loose and peeling material removed, then feather‑sanded so the transition doesn’t telegraph through the finish coat.

  • Repair rot and failed wood

    Soft trim, sills and fascia get replaced or filled properly. Painting over rot buys you one season and costs you the whole job.

  • Spot‑prime every bare surface

    Bare wood, bare metal and every repair gets the right primer for that substrate — stain‑blocking, bonding or rust‑inhibitive as required.

  • 05

    Caulk every open joint

    Elastomeric caulk at trim‑to‑siding, window and door surrounds and any gap water can find. This is what actually keeps the wall dry.

  • 06

    Mask and protect everything else

    Windows, roofing, hardware, lights, walks and every plant that isn’t getting painted. Drop cloths down, not newspaper.

  • 07

    Two full coats of finish

    Sherwin‑Williams, spread at the manufacturer’s rate — not stretched thin to save a bucket. Sprayed and back‑rolled where the substrate calls for it.

  • 08

    Walk it with you before you pay

    We go around the whole house together with a punch list. Anything you flag gets fixed before the balance is due.
