Read this before you accept any bid

Three contractors will walk your house and hand you three numbers. The numbers will not be close to each other, and the difference almost never comes down to greed. It comes down to what got left out of the cheap one.

This checklist gives you the twelve things that actually determine whether your paint lasts, and the exact question to ask about each. Take it with you to every walk-through — including ours.

What this climate does

Western Pennsylvania puts a specific set of stresses on an exterior finish. Understanding them is most of what you need to judge a bid.

The stressWhat it does to your house
Freeze–thaw cycling Water gets into an open joint, freezes, expands roughly 9%, and levers the coating off the substrate. Pittsburgh crosses freezing in both directions dozens of times a winter. This is the single biggest killer of exterior paint here.
Wet springs Substrate that never fully dries. Coating applied over damp wood blisters as the moisture tries to escape.
Mature tree canopy North elevations stay damp and shaded and grow mildew fast. Painting over mildew just feeds it under the film.
Road salt & grit Chews at the lower three feet of the house and anything near a driveway or steps.
A century of brick Old soft brick and lime mortar need to breathe. Seal them with the wrong coating and they spall — the face of the brick pops off. That damage is permanent.
Hillside exposure Half the city sits on a slope, so one elevation catches far more weather and UV than the others and ages twice as fast.

The short version

If you only remember one thing: ninety percent of exterior paint failure is prep failure, not paint failure. The product on the can matters far less than what happened to the surface in the three days before it went on. Every question in this checklist is really a question about prep.

The checklist · items 1–6Page 3
1

Is the house getting washed — and then allowed to dry?

Dirt, chalk, pollen and mildew all break adhesion. Washing is standard; the part crews skip is the drying. Substrate needs a genuine dry-out, not a few hours.

AskHow many days between the wash and the first coat?
2

How much scraping and sanding is included?

Loose material has to come off, and the hard edge left behind has to be feathered smooth or it telegraphs straight through the new finish.

AskIs scraping capped at a number of hours, or is it "until it's sound"?
3

Who is fixing the rot — and is it in this price?

Soft sills, fascia and trim will not hold paint. Painting over rot buys one season and costs you the whole job. It should appear as a visible line item, not a surprise later.

AskShow me the rot you found, and where the repair sits on this quote.
4

Is every bare surface getting primed?

Bare wood, bare metal and every repair need the right primer — stain-blocking, bonding or rust-inhibitive depending on what it's going over. "The paint is self-priming" is not an answer for bare substrate.

AskWhich primer, on which surfaces?
5

What exactly is getting caulked?

This is the freeze–thaw defence and the most commonly skipped step in the trade. Trim-to- siding joints, window and door surrounds, and every gap water can find.

AskWhich joints, and what grade of caulk?
6

One coat or two?

The most common hidden difference between two bids. One coat looks identical on handover day and fails in roughly half the time. Get the number in writing.

AskIs two coats written on the quote, or just discussed?
The checklist · items 7–12Page 4
7

Is the paint being spread at the rated thickness?

Every product has a manufacturer's spread rate. Stretching a bucket further is invisible on day one and is exactly how a "two coat" job performs like one.

AskHow many gallons are budgeted for this house?
8

If it's brick — is the coating breathable?

Old Pittsburgh brick must release moisture. A non-breathable film traps water, and the freeze–thaw cycle then pops the face off the brick. That is not repairable by repainting. Brick also needs repointing and full drying first.

AskWhich masonry system, and is my brick actually a candidate?
9

How is the north side being treated?

Shaded, damp elevations need mildewcide treatment during the wash, not a coat of paint over live growth.

AskWhat are you doing differently on the shaded elevations?
10

What's the plan when it rains?

A crew chasing a deadline through a wet forecast will coat damp substrate and it will blister. You want to hear that they stop and come back.

AskWhat's your minimum surface temperature and dry-time rule?
11

Same crew every day, or whoever's free?

Rotating subcontractors is where agreed details get lost. It is a quality question, not a nosy one.

AskWho specifically is on my property, and are they employees or subs?
12

Is there a written warranty — and proof of insurance?

A workmanship warranty should be on paper with a stated term. Ask every bidder for their certificate of insurance. Anyone who hesitates has told you something important.

AskCan I see the certificate and the warranty wording before I sign?

When to paint in Western PA

WindowVerdict
Late Apr – MayWorkable, but wet. Expect weather delays.
June – JulyFine, though high humidity slows cure and afternoon storms cost days.
Aug – OctThe best window. Lower humidity, more workable days, proper cure before the first freeze.
Nov – MarExteriors stop below roughly 50°F surface temp. The right time for interiors, cabinets and basements — and the easiest time to get on a schedule.

Five red flags on a bid

1. A single lump-sum number. If it isn't itemized you cannot tell what's missing, which is usually the point.

2. No mention of primer or caulk. The two steps that decide the lifespan.

3. A large deposit up front. A staged schedule tied to milestones is normal; most of the money before work starts is not.

4. Pressure to sign today. A real quote is still valid next week. Discounts that expire when the salesperson leaves your kitchen are a sales tactic.

5. Hesitation about insurance. If someone gets hurt on your property and the contractor isn't covered, that can land on your homeowner's policy.
