Common questions

Concrete & roofing questions, answered

Straight answers to what homeowners in Southwestern Pennsylvania ask us most.

What areas does Set 'N Concrete serve?

Set 'N Concrete serves Southwestern Pennsylvania, including Allegheny, Washington, Westmoreland, Butler, Beaver, Armstrong, Fayette and Greene counties. That covers Pittsburgh and surrounding communities such as Monroeville, Cranberry Township, Canonsburg, Greensburg, McMurray, Bethel Park and Uniontown. Call or text 412-439-8833 to confirm coverage for your address.

Are estimates really free?

Yes. Every estimate from Set 'N Concrete is free and carries no obligation. We come to the property, measure the area, check access and drainage, and leave you with a written scope and price. Call or text 412-439-8833 to schedule one.

What does a new concrete driveway cost?

Driveway cost depends on square footage, slab thickness, how much excavation and base work the site needs, whether an old slab has to be torn out and hauled away, and how easily a truck can reach the pour. Because those variables swing the number a great deal, we price each job on site rather than by a per-foot rule of thumb — and the estimate is free either way.

How long before I can walk or drive on new concrete?

As a general rule, you can walk on new concrete after about 24 to 48 hours, and drive a passenger vehicle on it after roughly 7 days. Keep heavy trucks, dumpsters and equipment off it for closer to 28 days, which is when a standard mix reaches its rated strength. We give you the exact timeline for your pour at the final walkthrough, since weather and mix design both shift it.

What is the difference between stamped and decorative concrete?

Decorative concrete is the umbrella term for any finish beyond plain gray — including dyes, swirl finishes, exposed aggregate and stamping. Stamped concrete is one type of decorative concrete: while the slab is still plastic, textured mats are pressed into the surface to imitate stone, slate, brick or wood plank. Both start with the same structural slab, so the choice is about appearance and budget rather than durability.

Is epoxy flooring a good choice for a Pennsylvania garage?

Yes — epoxy is particularly well suited to Pennsylvania garages because it seals the slab against road salt and snowmelt, which are the main causes of surface pitting and spalling here. A properly installed floor is mechanically ground first so the coating bonds to the concrete rather than sitting on top of it, and a flake broadcast adds traction for wet winter tires. Prep is what separates a floor that lasts from one that peels in two years.

Can concrete be poured in winter in Western PA?

Concrete can be poured in cold weather, but it needs cold-weather practices: a heated or accelerated mix, insulated blankets, and protection from freezing for the first several days. Flatwork is best scheduled between spring and late fall in our region, so most winter inquiries get scheduled for the first available window once the ground thaws. We'll tell you honestly whether your job should wait.

Do you handle excavation and site prep before the pour?

Yes. Set 'N Concrete runs its own excavation equipment, so digging, grading, base preparation and haul-away are part of the same job and the same estimate. That's the main advantage of hiring one company — the crew that grades the site is the crew that pours on it, so nobody is waiting on an outside operator's schedule.

Do you do both roofing and concrete on the same property?

Yes — that's the point of "one company, every solution." Set 'N Concrete handles concrete, decorative concrete, excavation, epoxy flooring, retaining walls and roof replacement, so a property that needs a new driveway and a new roof can be handled under a single estimate and a single schedule.

Are you insured?

Yes. Set 'N Concrete carries contractor's liability insurance, and proof of insurance along with our Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor registration is provided with every written estimate. Ask for it up front — you should ask any contractor for it.
