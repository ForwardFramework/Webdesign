The problem

Five assessments. Five reports. Nobody reads all five.

Buy the Big Five and you get traits with no job attached. Buy DISC and you get a four-letter label. Buy the reasoning test separately and it arrives in a different PDF, three days later, from a different vendor.

So the hiring manager reads the first page of the one that arrived first, and hires the person they already liked. The assessment did not change a single decision — it just added a step and an invoice.

This is one twenty-minute assessment scored five ways at once, delivered as a single profile that ends in a recommendation you can argue with in a debrief.

Quick answer

Two people with identical résumés fail in completely different ways. One because the seat needed patience they don't have. The other because it needed a fight they wouldn't pick.

What we score

Five instruments. One profile.

Every item feeds more than one framework. That is why twenty minutes produces what would otherwise take four separate tests and a fortnight of chasing vendors.

01 — OCEAN

Big Five

The research backbone. Five broad traits with the deepest published evidence base for predicting job performance.

Gives youOpenness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Emotional Stability — each built from three or four workplace facets.

02 — DISC

Working style

How the person shows up in a room. Scored from forced-choice blocks, so agreeing with everything cannot inflate it.

03 — HPI

Bright-side scales

How this person is described by the people around them on an ordinary day, at their best.

Behavioural drives

Four drives that tell you how to manage the person, plus the nearest of eighteen reference profiles.

Gives youDominance, Extraversion, Patience, Formality, and a reference profile such as Maverick or Guardian.

Job competencies

The performance layer, including a short abstract-reasoning section — the part most personality tests leave out entirely.

The method

Twenty minutes in. A decision-grade profile out.

01

Answer seventy items

Fifty-four statements about how you work, ten forced-choice word blocks, and six reasoning problems.

Progress saves as you go, so a closed tab costs nothing. No timer — but first instinct is usually the accurate one.

02

Scored five ways at once

Sixteen workplace facets are banded against norms, then composed into every framework above.

Every derived score is a published weighting of those facets. Nothing is a black box, and every weight can be re-tuned against your own hires.

03

Read the profile, run the interview

Role-fit scores, management notes, response-quality flags, and six interview questions written against this candidate.

The questions target the low scores worth verifying and the high scores worth confirming — not a generic competency list.

Read it honestly

A profile that tells you when not to trust it.

Most assessments hand back five confident numbers no matter how the questions were answered. This one checks the response set first and puts the verdict at the top of the report.

Candour catches a candidate who denies ordinary human failings. Consistency compares items that measure the same trait from opposite directions. Engagement catches straight-lining and a pace too fast to be considered.

When those checks trip, the report says the trait scores are a ceiling and should be probed in interview. It does not quietly report them as fact.

Where it belongs

  • Use it after a first conversation, to shape the interview and the reference calls.
  • Weigh it alongside work history and demonstrated results, never instead of them.
  • Share the profile with the candidate. It reads as development feedback, not a verdict.

Never screen anyone out on a score alone, use it as a pass/fail gate, or treat it as a clinical or diagnostic instrument. It is none of those things.

Questions

Answers, before you have to ask.

Is this the actual DISC, Hogan, PI or Caliper test?

No, and it does not claim to be. Those are proprietary, separately normed instruments owned by their publishers. This is an independent assessment built on the same public constructs — the five-factor model, the DISC quadrants, Hogan's bright-side scale structure, PI's four-drive framework, and Caliper's competency-plus-reasoning approach. Where you need a certified, legally defensible instrument for a specific hire, license the real one.

How long does it take?

About twenty minutes. There is no timer. Answers save to your browser as you go, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off.

Where does the data go?

Nowhere. Everything is scored in your browser — no server, no account, no database. Answers live in your own browser storage until you clear them, and a profile only leaves your machine if you export it or copy the share link yourself.

Can candidates game it?

Partly, and that is expected. The forced-choice section makes candidates commit to a preference rather than agreeing with everything, the candour scale catches the too-good-to-be-true response set, and a consistency check compares items measuring the same trait from opposite directions. When those trip, the report says so instead of reporting inflated scores as fact.

Are the norms real?

The norm table is illustrative — a documented set of means and standard deviations that spread scores realistically, not a validated sample. It lives in one file and is designed to be replaced. Once you have 150 or more respondents in a given role, swap in your own numbers and the percentile bands become genuinely meaningful for your firm.

Is it legal to use in hiring?

Personality assessment in hiring is regulated, and the rules vary by jurisdiction. This instrument deliberately contains no items about health, disability, religion, age, family status or any other protected characteristic — every item is about workplace behaviour. That said, it has not been validated for adverse impact. Use it to structure interviews and inform judgement, never as a pass/fail screen, and take local employment-law advice before making it a formal part of a hiring process.
