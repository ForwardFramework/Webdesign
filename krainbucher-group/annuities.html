Annuities

A paycheck that cannot outlive you.

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company: you hand over a sum, and the company takes on the one risk your portfolio cannot diversify away — the risk that you live a very long time. Used well, it can extend retirement cash flow 30+ years beyond what a 401(k), IRA or mutual fund is built to deliver on its own.

Guaranteed income for life

Contractual payments that continue for as long as you live, and, if you elect it, for as long as your spouse lives after you.

The four kinds worth knowing

Not one product. A category.

“Annuity” covers instruments as different as a savings certificate and a pension. Most of the bad press attaches to one branch of the family and gets applied to all of them.

Fixed

A declared rate, for a set term

The insurer credits a stated interest rate for a defined period. Predictable, simple, and the closest thing to a CD inside an insurance contract — with tax deferral a CD does not offer.

Best when: you want certainty over a defined horizon.

Fixed indexed

Upside linked to an index, floor at zero

Interest is credited based on the movement of an external index subject to a cap, spread or participation rate. A negative index year credits zero rather than a loss. Greater potential upside than a traditional fixed contract, with less risk than a variable one.

Best when: you want market-linked growth without market drawdown.

Immediate — SPIA

A premium in, a pension out

You exchange a lump sum for payments beginning almost immediately, for a set number of years or for life. The highest income per dollar, at the cost of giving up access to the principal.

Best when: you need income now and have other liquid assets.

Deferred income

Buy the income today, start it later

Payments are locked in now to begin at a future date. The longer the wait, the larger the eventual payment — often used to cover the later, more expensive decades of retirement.

Best when: you are 5–15 years out and want to pre-commit an income floor.

The other side of it

What an annuity is bad at.

We would rather you hear this from us than from a headline. An annuity is a specialised tool and there are several situations where it is simply the wrong one.

  • Liquidity. Most deferred contracts carry surrender charges for a set period. Money you may need next year does not belong in one.
  • Caps and participation rates. On an indexed contract these can change within contractual limits, and you do not receive dividends from the underlying index.
  • Complexity. Riders and bonuses have costs. If a feature cannot be explained in a sentence, it should be questioned.
  • Taxation of gains. Withdrawals of gain are taxed as ordinary income, and withdrawals before 59½ may carry a 10% federal penalty.
  • One carrier’s promise. Guarantees are backed by the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurer, which is why carrier strength is part of the recommendation, not a footnote.
A reasonable rule of thumb

Annuitise the floor. Invest the rest.

Add up the expenses you must cover every month regardless of markets — housing, food, healthcare, insurance. Cover that floor with guaranteed sources: Social Security, any pension, and if there is a shortfall, an annuity sized to close exactly that gap.

Everything above the floor can stay invested for growth, because a bad year no longer threatens the lights staying on. That is the whole argument for the product, and it is why we size contracts to a number rather than to a percentage of your savings.
