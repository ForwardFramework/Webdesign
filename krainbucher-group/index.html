02 — The gap

Your 401(k) was never designed to be an income plan.

It was designed to accumulate. Nothing in it tells you how much you can safely spend, what happens in a bad first year of withdrawals, or what the tax bill looks like when the deferred money finally comes out.

A properly built retirement has two halves: the pile you grow, and the paycheck you turn it into. Most people arrive at 62 with only the first half solved.

  • Tax deferred is not tax free — the balance you see is a pre-tax number.
  • Sequence-of-returns risk does the most damage in the first five years of withdrawals.
  • No component of a standard 401(k) is contractually obligated to pay you for life.

03 — How we work

“We help families set themselves up for a secure future in the event anything catastrophic happens.”

That sentence does a lot of quiet work. Catastrophic rarely means what people picture. More often it is a diagnosis at 54, a disability that ends an income five years early, or a surviving spouse who inherits a mortgage and a tax bill on the same afternoon.

Our job is to find those scenarios before they find you, put a number on each one, and then decide together which are worth transferring to an insurance company and which you can comfortably carry yourself.

04 — The process

Three meetings. No obligation in any of them.

Step one — 20 minutes

Discovery call

Who depends on your income, what is already in place, what keeps you up. No documents needed, and nothing is presented on this call.

Step two — the analysis

The gap, in writing

We put a number on the shortfall: what a survivor would actually receive, what the income plan produces, and what the tax exposure looks like.

Step three — your call

Options, side by side

Two or three routes from different carriers, with the trade-offs stated plainly. If the honest answer is that you don’t need anything, that is the answer you get.
