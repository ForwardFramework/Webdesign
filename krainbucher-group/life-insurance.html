Life Insurance

The cheapest thing you will ever buy for someone else.

Life insurance is the one purchase where the buyer never sees the benefit. It exists so the mortgage still gets paid, the tuition still gets funded, and a grieving spouse never has to make a financial decision in the first month.

The three shapes

Term, whole life, indexed. Different jobs.

The first question is never “which product”. It is “how long does this need to last”. A twenty-year need and a permanent need are not solved by the same contract.

Temporary need

Term

Coverage for a fixed number of years — typically 10, 20 or 30 — at the lowest cost per dollar of benefit. It pays if you die during the term and expires if you don’t.

  • Matches finite obligations: mortgage, child-raising years, a business loan.
  • Buys the largest benefit on a working family’s budget.
  • Many policies are convertible to permanent coverage later without a new medical exam.
Permanent need

Whole life

Coverage that protects the insured for their entire life and remains in effect as long as all premiums are paid. It costs more than term, and in exchange it offers benefits term does not.

  • Level premium and a guaranteed death benefit that does not expire.
  • Guaranteed cash value that builds over time and can be borrowed against.
  • Certainty over flexibility — the most predictable contract on the shelf.
Permanent + accumulation

Indexed universal life

Permanent coverage with a flexible premium and cash value credited on the movement of an index, subject to a cap or participation rate, with a floor that prevents index losses from reducing it.

  • The engine behind most tax-free retirement strategies.
  • Flexible premium and adjustable death benefit as circumstances change.
  • Requires funding discipline — an underfunded policy is the classic failure mode.

Policy loans and withdrawals reduce cash value and the death benefit, may cause a policy to lapse, and may create a taxable event. Guarantees are subject to the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurer.

Living benefits

A policy that pays while you are still here.

Modern policies can include riders that give you access to your benefits without paying a penalty for it. If you are diagnosed with a qualifying critical, chronic or terminal illness, part of the death benefit can be accelerated to you while you are alive.

That changes what the product is for. It is no longer only a bet on dying; it is a source of capital at exactly the moment income stops and expenses spike.

Accelerating a benefit reduces the amount payable at death. Riders vary by carrier and state, may carry a cost, and are subject to eligibility and underwriting.

More on living benefits
How much coverage — a starting frame

Add the obligations. Subtract what already exists.

  • Income replacement — the household’s share of your income, for the number of years it is genuinely needed.
  • Debt — mortgage balance, vehicles, business loans and any co-signed obligation.
  • Education — a realistic figure per child, not a national average.
  • Final expenses — funeral, medical balances and estate settlement costs.
  • Minus — existing policies, group coverage through work, and liquid savings.

Group coverage through an employer is the item most often over-counted. It is usually a multiple of salary, rarely portable, and it ends the day the job does.
