Tax-Free Retirement

Tax-deferred is not tax-free.

The balance on your 401(k) statement is a pre-tax number. Somewhere inside it is a bill, and neither you nor anyone else knows what rate it will be settled at. A tax-free retirement strategy simply makes sure that not every dollar you retire on is exposed to that unknown.

Review my tax exposure See annuities
The framework

Three buckets. Most people only fill one.

Every dollar you will retire on sits in one of three tax treatments. Diversifying across them is what gives you control over your taxable income later — and control is the whole objective.

Bucket 01

Tax-deferred

Traditional 401(k), 403(b), traditional IRA. A deduction today, ordinary income tax on every dollar withdrawn later, and required minimum distributions that eventually force the issue whether you need the money or not.

Bucket 02

Taxable

Brokerage accounts, savings, CDs. Fully liquid and flexible, but taxed as you go — interest, dividends and realised gains create a bill in years you may not have wanted one.

Bucket 03

Tax-free

Roth accounts, municipal bond interest and properly structured cash value life insurance. Funded with money the IRS has already been paid on, so future rate changes have nothing left to reach.

The vehicles

How the third bucket actually gets filled.

There is no single tax-free product. There are a handful of tools with specific rules, and the strategy is choosing among them in the right order for your income and timeline.

Tool 01

Roth contributions and conversions

The most direct route, and where we usually start. Conversions are most attractive in the low-income window between the last paycheck and the start of Social Security and required distributions. Coordinate this with your CPA — the conversion is a taxable event in the year it happens.

Tool 02

Properly structured cash value life insurance

An indexed universal life policy funded for accumulation can build cash value that is accessed later through withdrawals to basis and policy loans, which are generally not taxable while the policy remains in force. No income limits and no contribution cap in the Roth sense — but real costs, real funding discipline, and real consequences if the policy lapses with a loan outstanding.

Tool 03

Health savings accounts

The only account with a deduction going in and tax-free withdrawals coming out for qualified medical expenses — which are a certainty in retirement. Frequently underused as a long-term asset rather than a spending account.

Read this before anyone sells you a policy

Life insurance is a good tax tool and a bad savings account.

It works when it is funded consistently for years, structured deliberately for cash value rather than maximum death benefit, and left in force. It fails when it is oversold to someone whose budget cannot sustain the premium, or when a loan is taken and the policy is allowed to lapse — which can trigger a tax bill on gains at the worst possible time.

If you have not filled available Roth and employer-match capacity first, that is almost always the better dollar. We will tell you that even though there is no commission in it.
